How the calendar was calculated

Open items (issued on or before the as-of date and not yet paid by it) land on their due date plus the counterparty's expected days late (median); the range uses its 25th and 75th percentiles. Items without a due date are assumed due after the default terms. Dates before the as-of date (overdue items) are moved to the as-of date. A period's expected total holds the items whose expected date falls in it; the low total holds only items whose whole early–late window falls in it, and the high total every item whose window touches it (for credits, the reverse). Amounts are exact minor units.

How payment behaviour was calculated

Days late = paid date − due date, for items paid on or before the as-of date. Per counterparty: the median is the expected delay and the 25th–75th percentiles are the range (linear interpolation, rounded half up to whole days). A counterparty with fewer than the minimum number of paid items uses the median and range of all counterparties in the same direction; if that pool is also too small, items are assumed to be paid on the due date (0 days).

How this was calculated

Items are counted in the month of their issue date (billed, not paid). Only complete months count: from the month of the earliest item (or the given history start) up to the last month that ended on or before the as-of date. Month-over-month change compares the latest complete month with the one before it. The trailing band is the average, lowest and highest of the last 3 complete months; the average is rounded once to minor units. With fewer than 3 complete months no trend numbers are produced (not enough data).

How this was calculated

Billed unit prices are grouped by vendor, item, unit and currency (names compared case- and punctuation-insensitively) and ordered by date. Each price is compared with the previous one in its group; an alert fires when it rose by more than the threshold (default: any rise).

Spend drift

Not enough data — 3 of 4 months. Drift compares the latest complete month with the 3 before it, so no drift numbers are shown yet.

How this was calculated

For each counterparty and direction, the latest complete month (by issue date) is compared with the average of the complete months before it (default 3). An alert fires when the absolute change is more than the threshold (default 25%) of that average and at least the minimum change; a counterparty with a zero baseline alerts on any non-zero month. Needs the baseline months plus the latest month to be complete, otherwise the result is not enough data.
